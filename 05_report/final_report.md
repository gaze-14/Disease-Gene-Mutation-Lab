## Title

**Molecular Analysis of a Documented TP53 Mutation and Its Predicted Effects on the p53 Protein**

## Disease Background

TP53 is a tumor suppressor gene that plays an important role in protecting cells from becoming cancerous. The gene encodes the p53 protein, which responds to cellular stress such as DNA damage. When DNA damage occurs, p53 can activate processes such as cell-cycle arrest, DNA repair, cellular senescence, or apoptosis. These processes help prevent damaged cells from continuing to divide. Mutations in TP53 can reduce or alter the normal activity of p53. Because p53 is important for controlling cell growth and maintaining genomic stability, TP53 mutations are associated with many types of cancer. Germline TP53 mutations can also cause Li-Fraumeni syndrome, an inherited cancer predisposition syndrome.

## Gene and Normal Protein Function

The gene analyzed in this project is **TP53 (tumor protein p53)**, located on **chromosome 17p13.1** in humans. The analyzed reference transcript was **NCBI RefSeq NM_000546.6**, transcript variant 1. The TP53 gene encodes the **p53 tumor suppressor protein**, which is a transcription factor. The protein contains important functional regions involved in transcriptional activation, DNA binding, and oligomerization. Under normal conditions, p53 helps maintain genomic stability by responding to cellular stress and DNA damage. Once activated, p53 regulates the expression of genes involved in cell-cycle arrest, DNA repair, apoptosis, senescence, and other cellular responses. Through these functions, p53 helps prevent the accumulation of damaged cells and uncontrolled cell proliferation.

## Documented Mutation

The documented TP53 mutation analyzed in this project is:

**TP53 c.1010G>A

This mutation represents a substitution of one nucleotide, where **guanine (G)** is changed to **adenine (A)** at coding DNA position **1010**. The mutation is located within the coding region of the TP53 transcript and results in a change in the encoded amino acid. Therefore, it is classified as a **missense mutation**. The mutation does not add or remove nucleotides from the sequence, so the reading frame is not expected to shift. Instead, the nucleotide substitution changes one codon and may alter one amino acid in the p53 protein.

## Hypothesis

The hypothesis of this study is that the documented **TP53 c.1010G>A** mutation will cause a single amino acid substitution in the p53 protein without changing the overall reading frame or protein length. Because the affected amino acid is located within the functional region of p53, the mutation may alter the protein's structure or its ability to perform its normal tumor-suppressor functions. This may reduce the ability of p53 to regulate genes involved in cell-cycle control, DNA repair, apoptosis, and other cellular responses.

## Methods

The normal TP53 reference sequence was obtained from the **NCBI RefSeq database** using accession **NM_000546.6**. The coding region analyzed in the project corresponded to the TP53 transcript sequence used for the protein translation experiment. The normal TP53 nucleotide sequence was translated using the **Galaxy Transeq tool** with the standard genetic code and reading frame 1. The resulting protein sequence was examined to determine its amino acid length and terminal residues. For the artificial mutation experiment, the nucleotide corresponding to the documented TP53 mutation **c.1010G>A** was changed from G to A in the reference sequence. The mutated sequence was then translated using the same reading frame and genetic code used for the wild-type sequence. The wild-type and mutant protein sequences were compared to determine the amino acid change, protein length, and possible effects on protein function.

## Results

The normal TP53 transcript analyzed was **NM_000546.6**. The coding sequence used in the translation experiment was **1,182 bp** long. Translation in reading frame 1 produced a p53 protein containing **393 amino acids**, excluding the stop codon. The artificial mutation involved a single nucleotide substitution at coding position **1010**, changing **G to A**. Because only one nucleotide was substituted and no nucleotide was inserted or deleted, the reading frame was maintained. The mutation produced a change in one amino acid in the translated protein. The overall protein length remained **393 amino acids**, indicating that the mutation did not cause a frameshift or premature stop codon.

The most important difference between the wild-type and mutant proteins is therefore the amino acid substitution and its possible effect on p53 structure and function.

## Artificial Mutation Experiment

The artificial mutation experiment was performed by modifying the wild-type TP53 nucleotide sequence at the position corresponding to **c.1010G>A**. The original nucleotide at this position was **G**. It was changed to **A**, producing the mutant nucleotide sequence. The modified sequence was translated using the same settings as the wild-type sequence. The experiment showed that changing one nucleotide was sufficient to change one amino acid in the resulting protein. Since the mutation was a substitution rather than an insertion or deletion, the downstream reading frame remained unchanged. The resulting mutant protein therefore retained the same overall length of 393 amino acids. This experiment demonstrates how a single nucleotide substitution can produce a specific amino acid change without necessarily altering the entire protein sequence.

## Molecular Interpretation: Gene → Mutation → Protein → Cellular Effect → Phenotype

**Gene:**  
The TP53 gene encodes the p53 tumor suppressor protein, which regulates important cellular responses to DNA damage and other forms of cellular stress.

**Mutation:**  
The documented mutation **TP53 c.1010G>A** changes one nucleotide in the coding sequence from G to A.

**Protein:**  
The nucleotide substitution changes one codon and produces a missense amino acid substitution. The reading frame and overall protein length remain unchanged.

**Cellular Effect:**  
A change in an important region of p53 may affect its structure, DNA-binding activity, transcriptional regulation, or interaction with other molecules. If p53 activity is reduced or altered, cells with damaged DNA may have a reduced ability to stop cell-cycle progression, repair DNA damage, undergo apoptosis, or enter senescence.

**Phenotype:**  
Reduced or altered p53 tumor-suppressor activity can contribute to abnormal cell survival and uncontrolled cell proliferation. At the organismal level, TP53 mutations are associated with increased cancer susceptibility and, for certain germline mutations, inherited cancer predisposition such as Li-Fraumeni syndrome.

## Limitations

This analysis was primarily based on nucleotide sequence translation and comparison of wild-type and artificially mutated TP53 sequences. The experiment predicts possible molecular consequences from the sequence change but does not directly measure the three-dimensional structure, stability, DNA-binding activity, transcriptional activity, or cellular behavior of the mutant p53 protein. The artificial mutation experiment also does not reproduce all biological conditions inside a living cell. Additional laboratory experiments would be required to confirm whether the mutation actually changes p53 protein activity and how strongly it affects cellular processes. The predicted cellular and phenotypic effects should therefore be interpreted as molecular predictions supported by the known function of TP53 rather than as direct experimental measurements.

## Conclusion

This project analyzed the human **TP53** gene and the effects of the documented **c.1010G>A** mutation on its encoded p53 protein. The normal TP53 sequence was translated to produce a 393-amino-acid protein. Artificial introduction of the documented nucleotide substitution changed one nucleotide and produced a missense amino acid substitution while maintaining the reading frame and overall protein length. The results demonstrate how a single nucleotide change can alter the amino acid sequence of a protein without causing a frameshift. Because p53 is an important tumor suppressor involved in cell-cycle arrest, DNA repair, apoptosis, and cellular stress responses, changes in its functional regions may affect these cellular processes. Overall, the sequence analysis supports a molecular pathway in which a TP53 nucleotide mutation can produce a protein-level change that may alter tumor-suppressor activity and contribute to abnormal cellular behavior.
